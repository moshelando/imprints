NOTE 14REMOTE TRAVEL6 MIN

What “remote” actually means

Remote is not a mood. It is a set of practical changes once a road, clinic, or easy alternate route is no longer nearby.

We plan communications, route exits, weather margin, evacuation options, and group pace before departure. In the field, the point is not to make remoteness dramatic; it is to make the decisions around it ordinary and legible.

FIELD RULE: A plan is useful only if the group knows when to abandon it.
NOTE 21RIVER7 MIN

Reading a northern river

The easiest line changes with current, wind, depth, and what the bank is doing next.

Good river travel is repetitive observation: where the main current sits, what the wind is building, where eddies offer rest, what a bend hides, and whether landing now is easier than landing later. We would rather stop early than turn a camp choice into a rescue exercise.

NOTE 27WEATHER5 MIN

Weather has right of way

A published itinerary is a sequence of intentions. Visibility and wind decide which intentions survive the morning.

That does not mean every cloudy day changes a route. It means guides continually compare the cost of continuing, waiting, rerouting, or turning around. The most useful skill is not “pushing through.” It is recognizing when the landscape has changed the terms.

FIELD RULE: Save margin for the decision you did not know you would need.
NOTE 32GEAR8 MIN

What goes in the dry bag

Wet systems work when important things have layers of protection, not when one miracle bag is expected to save everything.

Sleep insulation and camp layers stay protected inside the main waterproof system. Daily rain gear stays reachable. Electronics get their own small dry layer. The goal is not to keep every object pristine; it is to keep the items that protect sleep, warmth, and communication reliably dry.
